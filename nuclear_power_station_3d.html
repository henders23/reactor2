Pressurised Water Reactor (PWR) Multi-Physics Context

A commercial nuclear power station is a coupled multi-physics converter spanning seven orders of magnitude in energy scale: translating quantum-mechanical nuclear binding energy (∼200 MeV per fission event) into bulk sensible enthalpy, high-enthalpy steam expansion, and finally synchronous 50 Hz three-phase electrical power on the grid.

1. Energy Density Disparity

Chemical combustion rearranges valence electrons (∼4 eV per reaction, ∼50 MJ/kg). Thermal fission of 235U rearranges nucleons bound by the strong force (∼202.5 MeV, ∼8.2 × 107 MJ/kg). One 10 g UO2 pellet produces the thermal energy of 1 tonne of coal.

2. Moderation & Self-Regulation

Fission emits fast neutrons (E ∼ 2 MeV). Ordinary light water acts as both coolant and moderator, elastically slowing neutrons to thermal equilibrium (0.025 eV) where the 235U fission cross-section is 500x higher. Thermal expansion provides an intrinsically negative moderator temperature coefficient of reactivity (αT < 0).

3. Three Isolated Loops

To safely contain neutron activation products (e.g. 16N), heat is stepped across three isolated closed fluid loops: (1) Primary loop at 15.5 MPa liquid, (2) Secondary Rankine cycle at 6.8 MPa steam, and (3) Tertiary open atmospheric heat sink via hyperbolic cooling towers.
